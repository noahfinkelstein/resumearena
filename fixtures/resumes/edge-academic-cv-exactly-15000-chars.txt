RESEARCH FIELDS
Public economics, labor economics and the economics of education. Empirical work on tax credits, social insurance and local public finance, mostly with linked administrative data and quasi-experimental designs built around state policy variation.

RESEARCH STATEMENT
My research asks how the design of taxes and transfers shapes work, mobility and local public finance, and it relies on administrative records that let these questions be answered at the level of the individual household or jurisdiction. One strand uses state variation in earned income tax credit supplements, unemployment insurance schedules and property tax limits to estimate behavioral responses with regression discontinuity and bunching designs. A second strand studies how programs interact: the marginal tax rates created by overlapping transfer phase-outs, the fiscal spillovers between neighboring jurisdictions, and the ways in which local governments substitute between revenue sources when one is capped. Current work extends this agenda to retirement savings mandates and place-based tax credits, and builds a linked employer-employee panel for Wisconsin with the Department of Revenue.
